# Prompt — design the interns' scope of work

Paste everything below the line into Claude (or any capable model). Update the
**Current position** numbers before re-running it, since the advice should shift
as those change.

---

You are advising the founder of a small creative-technology company on how to
structure the work of two graduate interns. Give direct, opinionated advice. If
something in my plan is unrealistic, say so plainly rather than accommodating it.

## The business

Digit One Zero — creative and media services (event production, video, live
streaming, conference production, exhibition booths). Based in Abuja, Nigeria.

- **Clients are mostly government and energy sector** — NNPC, PTDF, ministries,
  and companies around the oil and gas industry. Payment terms run about 30 days.
- Around 99% of work has historically come through referrals.
- The founder personally holds every client relationship. Freelancers can deliver
  the work; nobody else can win it. Clients want the founder in the room.
- Delivery crew is hired per project. There are **no permanent staff**.
- The company exhibits at industry events — NAICE, NOG and similar — which is
  where its buyers physically gather.
- The founder's proven opener: photograph and film people at these events, collect
  their contact details, send them the material free, then follow up. Value first,
  ask later.

## Current position

- 3 active projects worth ₦21.5M total
- ₦19.5M invoiced, ₦7.5M collected — the founder is handling collection personally
- 2 clients on the books, neither with a second known contact
- 0% of revenue under any recurring agreement
- The founder's stated goal is a business that can eventually run without him and
  be handed to his children

## The two interns

Both are on a 12-month NJFP graduate internship, working **Monday, Wednesday and
Thursday only** — three days a week, roughly 12–13 working days a month, about
144 working days across the entire internship.

**Arome — Operations & Growth.** Intended to grow into an Operations Coordinator.
The founder wants him functioning as an informal admin now, ahead of hiring for
that role properly. The founder is explicit that Arome is an intern and should not
be pushed too hard.

**Esther — Brand & Communications.** Handles social media, content and the
company's public image, plus building the founder's personal professional brand.

## Principles the plan must honour

1. **Narrow and deep beats broad and shallow.** An intern on three days a week
   cannot own four responsibility areas at once. Give each of them ONE process to
   own in month 1, add a second in month 2, a third in month 3. Do not produce a
   job description with percentage splits across many areas.
2. **Every owned process must be genuinely theirs** — a task they complete
   end-to-end without the founder finishing it off.
3. **Nothing that requires the client relationship**, because that cannot be
   delegated yet.
4. **Social media is not the growth engine for this buyer.** A government or
   energy-sector communications director does not hire a production company from
   an Instagram reel. What works is proof of relevant work, reputation, referral,
   and being on approved vendor lists. Content should make the event motion
   compound — case studies, founder LinkedIn, footage delivered to the people in
   it — rather than chase posting volume.
5. **LinkedIn before Instagram**, and the founder's own profile before the company
   page, because the founder is the relationship.
6. **Government buying runs on prequalification and published tenders.** Vendor
   registration, keeping statutory documents current, and monitoring tender
   portals is unglamorous, mechanical work that is well suited to an operations
   intern and puts the company where budget is actually spent.
7. **Targets must fit 12–13 working days a month.** If a deliverable count does
   not fit, say so and propose a realistic number instead of quietly accepting it.
8. Work should be **batched** — one day planning, one day producing, one day
   publishing — because context-switching destroys a three-day week.

## What to produce

For **each intern separately**:

1. **A phased ramp** — what they own in month 1, month 2, month 3, and what they
   grow into by months 6 and 12. One new owned process per month at most.
2. **A weekly rhythm** across Monday, Wednesday and Thursday, with each day having
   a single clear purpose.
3. **Monthly deliverables**, expressed as weekly numbers so nothing waits until
   month end.
4. **A definition of "done"** for each owned process — what the founder should be
   able to stop checking.
5. **A quarterly learning plan** across the 12 months, tied to what they are
   actually doing that quarter rather than generic training topics.
6. **Three to five KPIs maximum**, each one measurable without the founder
   manually assessing it.

Then, across both:

7. **What the founder must keep doing personally** and must not delegate yet.
8. **What to stop or postpone** — things in a conventional intern job description
   that would be a waste of these three days.
9. **A three-month review checkpoint**: what evidence would show this is working,
   and what would indicate the plan needs changing.

## Tone and constraints

- Be specific and concrete. "Research prospects" is useless; "identify and register
  on three government vendor portals this month" is actionable.
- Where a target is unrealistic on three days a week, say so and give the number
  you would use.
- Do not pad. No motivational language, no filler sections.
- If you think one of these two roles is aimed at the wrong outcome for this
  business, say that too.
