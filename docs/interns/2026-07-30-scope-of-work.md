**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Working days:** Monday, Wednesday, Thursday — 3 days/week, ~12–13 days/month,
~144 days across the 12-month internship
**Supersedes:** `2026-07-30-intern-learning-plans.md`, which was built from the
original job descriptions before the government-sector focus was clear.

## Two things I'd change before you read the rest

**Arome's job description is aimed at the wrong outcome.** It allocates 30% to
"Business Development" — researching prospects, building pipeline, identifying
partnerships. In a business where *you* are the relationship and clients want you
in the room, an intern cannot do business development. What he can do is
**market access**: getting the company registered where government budget is
released, and watching for tenders. Same intent, achievable version.

**Esther's is closer, but measures the wrong thing.** Twelve posts a month is a
volume target. Your buyer is a government or energy-sector communications
director who will not hire you from a reel. Her role should be a **proof engine** —
case studies, the founder's LinkedIn, and turning event footage into assets that
make a procurement officer comfortable choosing you.

Both revised below.

# AROME — Operations & Market Access

## Phased ramp — one new owned process per month

| Month | New process he owns | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Vendor registration & statutory compliance** | Government work is bought through prequalification. Nothing else he does matters if you can't be awarded. |
| **2** | **System of record** — everything entered into DOZ OS | You won't log invoices at 9pm. He can on Monday. Every metric is worthless without this. |
| **3** | **Tender & opportunity monitoring** | Once registered, watch what's published. Needs the sector knowledge months 1–2 give him. |
| **6** | **Proposal and bid assembly** from templates | Needs to have seen 3 months of real tenders first. |
| **12** | Coordinates one small client project end-to-end; runs the weekly operations meeting | The Operations Coordinator outcome |

Nothing else is added. Three owned processes by month 3 is a real contribution;
six would be none.

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | Purpose | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Operations | Enter last week's projects/invoices/expenses into DOZ OS. Update project status. Agree the week's priorities with you. |
| **Wednesday** | Market access | Vendor portal work — registrations, renewals, document uploads. Review published tenders. Log relevant ones. |
| **Thursday** | Documentation & review | Weekly report. Mentoring session. File and organise project documents. |

Daily report at the end of every working day: **DOZ OS → Field Mode → File Daily Report.**

## Monthly deliverables, as weekly numbers

| Deliverable | Monthly | Weekly | Day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vendor portals registered (months 1–3) | 3 | — | Wednesday |
| Registrations kept current / renewed | all | check | Wednesday |
| Tenders reviewed | 8–12 | 2–3 | Wednesday |
| Tenders flagged as relevant with a one-paragraph summary | 2–3 | — | Wednesday |
| Projects/invoices/expenses entered | 100%, within 3 working days | ongoing | Monday |
| Weekly report | 4 | 1 | Thursday |
| Daily reports | 12–13 | 3 | every day |

**Dropped from the original JD:** "20 new companies researched" and "10 qualified
prospects" monthly. For government buying, cold company research produces almost
nothing — the budget moves through procurement portals and published tenders.
Registration and tender monitoring replace it, and are a better use of the same
Wednesdays.

## Definition of done — what you stop checking

- **Vendor registration:** you never again ask "are we registered with X?" or
  discover a tax clearance expired. He tells you before it lapses.
- **System of record:** you can open DOZ OS on any Friday and the numbers are true
  without you verifying them.
- **Tender monitoring:** you never find out about a relevant tender after it closed.

## Learning plan — tied to what he's actually doing

**Q1 (Months 1–3) — The sector and the system**
- How Nigerian public procurement works: BPP, due process, prequalification, the
  statutory documents (CAC, tax clearance, PENCOM, ITF, NSITF) and why each exists
- DOZ OS end to end: projects, invoices, expenses, CRM
- Business writing: professional email, status updates, meeting minutes
- *Course:* project management fundamentals
- *Session:* present "how a government contract gets awarded" to you

**Q2 (Months 4–6) — Process**
- Turning something you do from memory into a written process
- Project coordination: schedules, dependencies, what slips and why
- Vendor and logistics coordination on live events
- *Course:* business operations or process improvement
- *Session:* present the first documented process and what it changed

**Q3 (Months 7–9) — Bids**
- Reading a tender properly: scope, evaluation criteria, compliance requirements
- Proposal structure; how pricing is presented; capability statements
- Why bids lose
- *Course:* proposal or bid writing
- *Session:* walk through a real bid, won or lost, and why

**Q4 (Months 10–12) — Independence**
- Prioritising without instruction
- Running a meeting
- Coordinating a project end to end
- *Course:* leadership or professional communication
- *Session:* propose an operational improvement with a business case

## KPIs — five, all measurable without you assessing anything

1. **Daily reports filed** — 3/week *(DOZ OS, automatic)*
2. **Weekly report submitted every Thursday** *(DOZ OS, automatic)*
3. **Data currency** — every project, invoice and expense entered within 3 working days
4. **Live vendor registrations** — count going up, expired count at zero
5. **Tenders reviewed** — 8+/month, with 2–3 flagged and summarised

# ESTHER — Brand & Proof

## Phased ramp

| Month | New process she owns | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Case studies from completed work** | You have 3 real projects sitting undocumented. This is the highest-value asset she can make, and it's the thing procurement officers actually read. |
| **2** | **The founder's LinkedIn** | You are the relationship. Your credibility is what transfers to the company. Higher leverage than the company page. |
| **3** | **Event capture & delivery** — the NAICE/NOG motion | Shoot people, get them their footage, follow up. Your proven opener, made systematic. |
| **6** | Company channels on a sustainable cadence | Only once there's proof material to post |
| **12** | Plans and runs a full campaign around an event | The Brand & Communications Officer outcome |

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | Purpose | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Writing | Case study drafting, your LinkedIn post, captions for the week |
| **Wednesday** | Production | Graphics, video editing, shooting. Everything visual, batched. |
| **Thursday** | Publishing & portfolio | Schedule the week's content. Update portfolio for completed projects. Weekly report. Mentoring. |

The batching rule matters more than the schedule: **never produce one post
end-to-end in a sitting.** The setup cost is what destroys throughput on three days.

## Monthly deliverables — revised, with the reasoning

| Deliverable | Original JD | **Revised** | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company social posts | 12 | **8** (2/week) | 12 originals doesn't fit 12 days alongside everything else |
| Short-form videos | 4 | **2** | Editing is the slowest task she has |
| Client case study | 1 | **1** | Keep — highest value item |
| Blog article | 1 | **1 per quarter** | Monthly blog + monthly case study is duplicate long-form |
| Founder LinkedIn posts | 4 | **4** (1/week) | Keep — highest leverage |
| Founder thought-leadership article | 1 | **1 per quarter** | Monthly is not sustainable at quality |
| Behind-the-scenes story | 1 | **1** | Captured on project days, near-zero marginal cost |
| Portfolio updated | ≤7 days after project | **Keep** | |

**The honest arithmetic:** the original list is ~23 finished pieces across ~12
working days — roughly two per day on top of shooting and editing. The revised
list is ~16, still demanding. If she consistently beats it in months 1–3, raise
the numbers. Don't start above what's achievable; a target nobody hits stops
being a target.

## Definition of done

- **Case studies:** you can send a procurement officer a document that explains
  what you did for NNPC or PTDF without writing anything yourself.
- **Founder LinkedIn:** something goes out every week that sounds like you, and
  you spend under 15 minutes on it.
- **Event capture:** everyone photographed at an event has received their material
  within 5 days, with their contact logged in the CRM.

## Learning plan

**Q1 (Months 1–3) — Craft and brand**
- Canva to a confident standard; brand consistency rules
- Copywriting: captions, headlines, calls to action
- Case-study structure: problem → approach → result
- Smartphone videography and CapCut basics
- AI tools for drafting, with the rule that **nothing publishes unedited**
- *Course:* copywriting or content marketing
- *Session:* present the brand guidelines she'll work to

**Q2 (Months 4–6) — Storytelling**
- Brand positioning; how a corporate buyer perceives a supplier
- Photography during live production
- Writing for the founder's voice as distinct from the company's
- *Course:* brand strategy or storytelling
- *Session:* present the first three case studies and what made them persuasive

**Q3 (Months 7–9) — Distribution**
- LinkedIn algorithm and B2B distribution — not consumer social
- SEO fundamentals for a services company
- Analytics: what to measure, what to ignore
- Email and newsletter basics
- *Course:* digital marketing or analytics
- *Session:* present 3 months of data with recommendations

**Q4 (Months 10–12) — Ownership**
- Campaign planning end to end
- PR and press releases
- Presentation design and public speaking
- *Course:* campaign management or PR
- *Session:* present a campaign she designed and ran

## KPIs — five

1. **Daily reports filed** — 3/week *(DOZ OS, automatic)*
2. **Weekly report submitted every Thursday** *(DOZ OS, automatic)*
3. **1 case study published per month**
4. **4 founder LinkedIn posts per month**
5. **Portfolio updated within 7 days** of every completed project

Deliberately **not** a KPI: follower count, likes, engagement rate. None of them
predict a government contract. Track **enquiries by source** in the CRM instead —
if social produces no enquiries in 3 months while events and referrals produce all
of them, move her effort further toward proof and events.

# Across both

## What you must keep doing personally

- Every client conversation and every room
- All pricing and negotiation
- Collections — government clients on 30-day terms need your standing
- Final approval on anything client-facing before it goes out
- The weekly mentoring session with each of them. **This is the one thing that
  cannot slip.** On three days a week, an unmentored intern is an idle intern.

## Stop or postpone

- **Cold company research** (Arome) — wrong mechanism for government buying
- **SOP writing** (Arome) — the SOP module in DOZ OS is read-only; he literally
  cannot do it. Postpone until it's built.
- **Partnership identification** (Arome) — needs relationships he doesn't have
- **Running operations meetings** (Arome) — month 10+, not month 1
- **12 posts/month** (Esther) — revised to 8
- **Monthly blog + monthly thought-leadership article** (Esther) — quarterly each
- **Paid campaign preparation** (Esther) — nothing to amplify yet

## Three-month checkpoint

**Working if:**
- Both filing 3 daily reports a week without being chased
- Arome: 3+ vendor registrations live; you've stopped asking about compliance status
- Arome: DOZ OS numbers true enough that you check them instead of your own memory
- Esther: 3 case studies exist; your LinkedIn has posted weekly without your effort
- At least one enquiry traceable to something they produced

**Needs changing if:**
- Either is waiting on you to unblock most tasks — the process isn't really theirs
- Targets are being hit but nothing changes commercially — measuring the wrong things
- Daily reports have lapsed — the habit failed and everything above it is guesswork
